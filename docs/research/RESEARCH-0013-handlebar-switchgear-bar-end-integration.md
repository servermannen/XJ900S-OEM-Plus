# RESEARCH-0013: XJ900S handlebar, switchgear and bar-end integration

**Purpose:** Bring together handlebar, switchgear, control-stack, locating-feature,
bar-end, and mirror-adapter evidence for later integration review without
selecting or accepting a complete handlebar/control system.

**Document status: Draft**

**Status: Unverified**

**Review: Technical Review Required**

## Research question

What evidence is required to establish whether the owner-reported FZS600
handlebar, Tracer-family left switch candidate, Tracer 9 right switch / DBW grip
candidate, MT-07 front master-cylinder candidate, clutch-perch candidate,
bar-end interface, and mirror-adapter arrangement can be integrated on the
XJ900S without compromising steering, rider-control operation, routing,
serviceability, or future electrical architecture boundaries?

## Scope

### Included

- Handlebar identity and direct physical observations.
- Clamp compatibility and straight control-section layout.
- Switch locating-pin and locating-hole geometry.
- Switchgear orientation and installed control-stack order.
- Master-cylinder, lever, clutch-perch, grip, throttle/grip and switch placement.
- Cable, hose, harness and wiring routing through full steering movement.
- Bar-end, internal thread, washer/spacer, mirror-adapter and mirror-retention
  interfaces.
- Powdercoat and finish considerations that affect inspection, fit or corrosion.
- Steering clearance, ergonomics, electrical compatibility boundaries and DBW
  architecture boundaries.
- Validation gates required before any final acceptance.

### Excluded

- Selection or acceptance of the complete handlebar/control system.
- Authorization to drill, weld, grind, bend, powdercoat, install, energize, road
  test or otherwise modify safety-critical controls.
- Supersession of [COMP-0003](../components/COMP-0003-2003-fzs600-handlebar.md),
  [COMP-0004](../components/COMP-0004-2023-2024-tracer9-right-switch-dbw-grip.md),
  or [COMP-0008](../components/COMP-0008-tracer-900-gt-left-handlebar-switch.md).
- New DBW architecture, Level 1 / Level 2 authority decision, switch electrical
  implementation decision or mirror legal-compliance claim.
- Brake-system acceptance, clutch-system acceptance, or final road-use
  validation.

### Left-switch locating observation

The project observation is that the Tracer left switch locating pin does not
align with the existing handlebar locating hole. Using the existing hole would
leave the switch approximately 45 degrees incorrectly rotated according to
owner observation.

The approximately 45 degree value is retained only as owner observation, not a
precision angular measurement. Previously discussed options included drilling a
new locating hole and welding the old hole closed before refinishing or
powdercoat. Neither option is accepted or authorized by this record.

## Clamp and control geometry requirements

Nominal handlebar diameter near 22 mm is not enough to establish compatibility.
A switch, master cylinder or clutch perch clamping on the bar is also not
acceptance. Direct measurement is required for:

- Handlebar clamp-region diameter.
- XJ clamp diameter.
- Clamp contact width.
- Clamp spacing.
- Handlebar straightness.
- Bar rotation reference.
- Left straight control length.
- Right straight control length.
- Bend start positions.
- Switch clamp widths.
- Master-cylinder clamp width.
- Clutch-perch clamp width.
- Grip lengths.
- Switch locating-pin positions.
- Required lever angles.
- Usable region before bends.
- Interference between adjacent controls.

The installed layout shall be documented with datums so later reviewers can
reproduce orientation and spacing checks.

## Locating-pin and locating-hole structural gate

**Status: Unverified**

No new locating-hole drilling is authorized by this research record. No welding
is authorized by this research record.

Before drilling any new locating hole, require:

- Exact desired switch orientation.
- Locating-pin diameter.
- Locating-pin protrusion length.
- Existing hole diameter.
- Existing hole axial position.
- Existing hole circumferential position.
- Proposed new-hole position.
- Edge distance to existing hole.
- Distance to handlebar bend / transition.
- Handlebar OD.
- Wall thickness.
- Material / construction if identifiable.
- Local damage/corrosion inspection.
- Effect on control clamp engagement.
- Effect on wiring exit and routing.
- Structural review.

No minimum edge distance, hole diameter or acceptable hole pattern is invented
by this record.

Before welding any existing hole, require:

- Verified handlebar material.
- Wall thickness.
- Insert/internal hardware location.
- Weld-process suitability.
- Heat-affected-zone assessment.
- Distortion risk.
- Residual stress / fatigue implications.
- Post-weld inspection method.
- Surface treatment implications.
- Technical review.

Welding a steering-control handlebar solely to hide an unused locating hole is
not automatically preferable to leaving the unused hole in place.

## Switchgear orientation

Switchgear orientation must be set by rider access, control-function
identification, lever clearance, harness exit direction, clamp engagement and
steering clearance, not by forcing a candidate switch into an existing locating
hole. The left-switch approximately 45 degree misorientation observation does
not by itself define the correct new hole position, switch rotation or final
control-stack sequence.

## Throttle / DBW boundary

The right-hand assembly is safety-critical. This record keeps two paths
separate:

1. Mechanical/physical installation of the right-hand control assembly.
2. DBW/APS electrical and functional integration.

Physical fit does not accept DBW. This record does not authorize powered DBW
operation and does not create a new DBW architecture decision.

Before any DBW acceptance, require:

- Exact purchased-candidate identity.
- Exact APS connector/pinout.
- Supply/reference.
- APS channel transfer functions.
- Channel correlation.
- Mechanical return.
- Overtravel behavior.
- Plausibility.
- Fault handling.
- Safe state.
- Level 1 ECU interface.
- Technically reviewed validation plan.

[ADR-0002](../decisions/ADR-0002-three-level-control-architecture.md) and the
[system architecture](../architecture/system-architecture.md) retain fuel,
ignition and any future accepted direct throttle authority in Level 1. Level 2
must not gain hidden throttle authority.

Full lock-to-lock steering checks and relevant suspension movement checks are
required for:

- Front brake hose.
- Clutch cable or future alternative.
- Switch harnesses.
- DBW/APS harness if later used.
- Indicator wiring if routed nearby.
- Any accessory wiring.

Checks shall include:

- Tension.
- Pinch.
- Rubbing.
- Minimum bend concerns.
- Connector strain.
- Reservoir/hose interference.
- Tank contact.
- Fairing contact.
- Instrument contact.
- Fork contact.
- Steering-stop interference.

No routing is accepted merely because the controls function at straight-ahead
steering.

## Bar-end and mirror interface

The bar-end and mirror interfaces require verification of:

- Actual handlebar end OD.
- Internal M6 thread major diameter, pitch, measured threaded depth, usable
  engagement depth and
  condition.
- Insert projection relative to tube end.
- Approximately 11 mm internal counterbore depth.
- Approximately 25 mm internal / continued through-hole depth.
- End-face geometry.
- Adapter shoulder/contact face.
- Adapter concentricity.
- M8 bolt engagement.
- Mirror adapter M16 interface if used.
- Washer/spacer interface.
- Tightening load path.
- Bottoming risk.
- Whether the bolt clamps the adapter or bottoms in the insert.
- Rotational retention.
- Mirror adjustment range.
- Brake/clutch lever clearance.
- Throttle-side free return.
- Steering/fairing/tank clearance.

Adequate thread engagement is not inferred merely because the bolt tightens.
The relationship between M6 threaded depth, the approximately 11 mm
counterbore, the approximately 25 mm continued/internal hole, insert geometry
and bolt bottoming still requires direct dimensional verification. Usable M6
thread engagement depth and bolt bottoming are separate measured quantities.
Mirror adapter loading shall not preload or distort the throttle grip. On the
throttle side, a free-return check is required after final bar-end torque.

The custom adapter is not accepted by this record. If it carries the mirror,
material identification and structural/retention review are required before
acceptance.

## Finish and powdercoat considerations

Relevant engineering concerns:

- Coating must not hide cracks, corrosion, deformation or other damage.
- Clamp and electrical/contact surfaces may need masking depending on the final
  design.
- Coating thickness can change control, clamp and bar-end fits.
- Threaded/internal interfaces must remain serviceable.
- Locating holes and edges require corrosion protection.

No coating process is approved by this record. This record does not invent
powdercoat preparation or cure-temperature requirements. Welding before
powdercoat is not assumed desirable.

## Steering clearance and ergonomics

Required checks:

- Full left and right steering movement to stops with rider controls installed.
- Clearance to tank, fairing, screen, instruments, forks and other adjacent
  hardware.
- Lever reach, lever sweep, switch access and engine-stop access with the
  controls in intended rider positions.
- Reservoir orientation and brake-hose exit through steering movement.
- Clutch cable free movement and required free play through steering movement.
- Throttle/grip free movement and return with adjacent controls and bar-end
  hardware tightened.

Ergonomic preference is not allowed to override steering clearance, throttle
return, braking access, clutch operation, structural integrity or serviceability.

## Electrical compatibility

Physical control placement does not establish electrical compatibility.
Required future electrical evidence includes connector identity, cavity
orientation, mating housings, terminal family, pinout, switch/contact or
communication behavior, supply/reference needs, protection, grounding, strain
relief, harness length and fault behavior. The switch assemblies shall not be
energized merely because their clamps, pins or connectors appear to fit.

## Analysis

The current evidence is enough to define an integration research and validation
campaign, but not enough to select the handlebar/control system. The available
records preserve purchased-candidate provenance, some owner/project physical
observations and architecture boundaries. They do not establish:

- Official Yamaha application of the FZS600 handlebar candidate or
  `5RT-26110-01`.
- Exact Yamaha application or supersession relationship for the left switch
  IDs `B1J-83954-11-00` and `B5U-83954-01`.
- Exact Yamaha application or relationship for the right switch IDs
  `BAP-8291R-10` and `B5U-8291R-10`.
- Structural suitability of the handlebar after existing or future locating
  features.
- Compatibility of control stack order, master cylinder, clutch perch,
  switchgear, grips, bar ends, mirrors, hoses, cables and harnesses.
- DBW/APS electrical or functional compatibility.
- Legal mirror compliance or road-use readiness.

The left-switch locating-pin conflict is a real integration concern, but it is
not enough evidence to authorize drilling or welding. The bar-end and custom
adapter observations are useful starting measurements, but bolt tightening is
not proof of thread engagement, clamping load path, rotational retention or
fatigue suitability.

## Conclusion

The handlebar, switchgear, locating-feature and bar-end integration remains
Unverified and requires technical review before final acceptance. This record
creates evidence gates only. It does not accept any component combination,
authorize locating-hole drilling, authorize welding, approve powdercoat,
accept mirror retention, accept the MT-07 master cylinder on this handlebar,
accept clutch-perch installation, accept DBW, or authorize road use.

- What authoritative evidence, if any, verifies the official Yamaha application

- What are the exact handlebar OD, clamp-region diameter, wall thickness,

- What is the exact desired switch orientation and locating-pin geometry for
  each switch?
- Does any proposed new locating hole meet structural review after real
  measurements, or should the unused old hole remain unused and protected?

  fatigue review?
- What installed left/right control sequence gives full clamp engagement,
  rider access, lever sweep, reservoir orientation and routing clearance?
- Does the bar-end/mirror adapter clamp through the intended load path without
  bottoming, loosening or interfering with throttle return?
- What are the exact switch connector identities, pinouts and electrical
  behaviors?
- What DBW/APS architecture, if any, will be proposed under a separate reviewed
  Level 1 decision?
